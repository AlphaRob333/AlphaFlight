Your path to the certificate

The typical order of milestones from first lesson to temporary certificate.

  1. Student certificate

    Apply through IACRA with your CFI. You must be at least 16 to solo.

  2. Medical

    Get a third-class medical (or higher) from an AME before your first solo.

  3. First solo

    Pre-solo knowledge test, then 61.87 training and a CFI endorsement.

  4. Cross-country

    Dual and solo cross-country, night training, and towered airport work.

  5. Knowledge test

    60 questions, 2.5 hours, 70% to pass. Needs a CFI endorsement.

  6. Checkride

    Oral and flight test with a DPE, flown to the ACS standards. You must be 17.

Questions

Common questions from student pilots starting out.

How long does a private pilot certificate take?

The regulation sets 40 hours as the minimum, but most students finish between 55 and 75 hours. Flying two or three times a week keeps skills fresh and usually costs less overall than flying once a week.

Does this course give me a knowledge test endorsement?

No. An endorsement for the FAA knowledge test must come from an authorized instructor, either your CFI after a ground review or a home-study course provider that issues endorsements. Use this course to prepare, then ask your CFI.

When should I take the knowledge test?

Many students take it around their solo cross-country phase, once the ground material lines up with what they are flying. Results are valid for 24 calendar months, so don't take it too early.

What medical do I need?

You need at least a third-class medical certificate to solo. BasicMed requires having held a medical certificate after July 14, 2006, so most new students start with a third-class medical from an AME.
